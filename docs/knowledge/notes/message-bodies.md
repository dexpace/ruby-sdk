## Conflicts
- **The I/O-layer ownership-on-wrap rule's surviving normative home is `IO-6`, a live MUST — not `SEAM-3`, which is retired.** Resolves `message-bodies/8a1e7a7b` ("The port defines two deliberately different ownership rules: at the I/O layer, wrapping takes ownership so closing a BufferedSource built over a caller's IO closes that IO, while at the body layer a body closes exactly the sources it opened itself. (SEAM-3, BODY-11, BODY-8)"), whose statement of the rule is correct and whose citation is not. `docs/sdk-design-ruby/10-deliberate-deviations-from-the-reference-contract.md` item 1 retires `SEAM-3`–`SEAM-10` with the byte-stream provider seam, and phase 2 shipped `SEAM-3` and `SEAM-4` as 🚫 with the reason attached — so a phase auditing that retirement and finding no surviving citation of the ownership rule could reasonably conclude the rule retires with the seam. **It does not.** `IO-6` (MUST) is its home: "When a provider wraps a caller-supplied underlying stream (readable stream -> buffered source, writable stream -> buffered sink), the returned wrapper MUST take ownership of that stream: closing the wrapper closes the underlying stream. The same holds for the stream bridges obtained from a buffered source/sink (closing the bridge closes the owning source/sink). Wrapping a plain byte array owns no external resource." `docs/sdk-design-ruby/10-…md` item 12 depends on it without naming it ("ownership-on-wrap remains the I/O layer's rule and is deliberately not the body layer's"), and `BODY-8` is the other half of that one decision. The sharper half of the finding, recorded as `OI-2` in `docs/open-items.md`: **`IO-6` appears in no specification chapter and in no design chapter at all** — outside `docs/product-spec/appendix-c-consolidated-normative-requirement-index.md` the string occurs once in the whole tree, in the roadmap's own gap paragraph, which sends a reader to `docs/product-spec/05-i-o-contracts.md`, a chapter that does not carry it; only the *bridge* half survives there, under `IO-16`, a SHOULD. Losing the rule would leave every `BufferedSource` built over a caller's stream leaking that stream on close, which no gate would catch. **What phase 3a does, and what a later reader should copy:** it reads `IO-6` out of appendix C, states the rule once, and cites `IO-6` and design §10.12 rather than `SEAM-3` — three factories carry it, `.wrapping` owning the stream, `Buffer.new` and `.of_bytes` owning no external resource (`IO-6`'s own last sentence), and there is deliberately **no borrowing variant**, because a wrapper that did not close what it wrapped is the exact object the MUST prohibits. `message-bodies/f060d944` remains correct and is the one stated exception: `BufferedSource.over` takes no ownership. A third ownership rule exists and belongs to neither layer — `SEAM-20`/`SEAM-21`/`SERDE-3` (`serde/cfbe4e9a`), where a codec closes nothing — and phase 3 neither implements nor weakens it.
